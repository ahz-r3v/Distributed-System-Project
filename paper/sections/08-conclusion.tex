\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The completed adapted replication shows that predictive eviction's effect depends on the scheduler.
Across all five coverage settings, prefix-aware LRU has the lowest mean session serving time, while adding predictive eviction increases that mean despite improving FCFS.
Full eviction events and reported uncached usage support the setting-level ordering, but request tails expose a separate responsiveness tradeoff.
The results apply to the selected runtime-bounded workloads and retain one preemption with unmeasured extra prefill work.
They motivate measuring recovery alternatives; they do not establish that secondary-storage restoration is cheaper than recomputation.
The separate Mini-SGLang study shows that retaining evicted KV in pinned host DRAM can avoid most recovery work for this long-prefix replay.
With LRU+FCFS fixed, restoration improves mean session time by 9.1$\times$ at $N=28$ and 4.8$\times$ at $N=36$ while adding a 37.5~GiB host tier.
Repeated $N=36$ runs give CacheWise-Fetch a further 1.23$\times$ advantage over CacheWise offload without fetch-aware ordering; predictive prefetch moves only about 2\% of restored tokens.
The implementation and evidence support GPU--host recovery, not SSD recovery.
Secondary-storage benefit remains contingent on implementing bounded staging and persistent blocks and measuring complete read, write, and queueing costs against recomputation.
