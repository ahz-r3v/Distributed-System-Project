\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The completed adapted replication shows that predictive eviction's effect depends on the scheduler.
Across all five coverage settings, prefix-aware LRU has the lowest mean session serving time, while adding predictive eviction increases that mean despite improving FCFS.
Full eviction events and reported uncached usage support the setting-level ordering, but request tails expose a separate responsiveness tradeoff.
The results apply to the selected runtime-bounded workloads and retain one preemption with unmeasured extra prefill work.
In the separate Mini-SGLang artifact, retaining evicted KV in pinned host DRAM is associated with substantially less missing-prefix work for the tested long-prefix replay.
With LRU+FCFS fixed, reported baseline-to-restoration session-time ratios are approximately 9.1$\times$ at $N=28$ and 4.8$\times$ at $N=36$, with an additional nominal 36~GiB host KV tier.
Two $N=36$ observations per configuration favor CacheWise-Fetch over predictive prefix scheduling without fetch-aware ordering, with an approximately 1.23$\times$ ratio between reported means.
Most configurations have one observation; the findings remain descriptive and do not isolate every mechanism or demonstrate equal-total-memory gains.
The completed scope is replication and GPU--host recovery with fetch-aware scheduling.
Persistent secondary storage remains future work, with no measured SSD speedup or break-even boundary claimed.
