Coding-agent serving repeatedly reuses long prefixes under limited accelerator memory.
We evaluate CacheWise scheduling and eviction mechanisms using synthetic-token replays of released traces on a single-GPU, 14B-model testbed.
An initial six-policy experiment is followed by 60 runs across nested $N=10,20,30$ workloads and two additional held-out cohorts.
Prefix-aware LRU has the lowest mean session serving time in all five coverage settings; adding predictive eviction increases that mean by 10.07--43.38\%, although predictive eviction helps FCFS.
Full eviction-event counts and completed-request uncached usage follow the same setting-level ordering.
Lower means do not ensure lower request tails.
One preemption is retained under a declared audit amendment, and its extra prefill work is unmeasured.
These adapted-replication results motivate a separate comparison of prefix recovery by recomputation and secondary-storage restoration.
